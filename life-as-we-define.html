2:50 AM, a Friday

Life as we define…or do we?!

Manish Kumar Sahoo

I was at Inorbit Mall today with my office colleagues because it so happens that I am the initiator and try to pull along as many people as I can, and being blessed with some of the best colleagues I ever had helps these impromptu plans come into action. In between, I have a habit of being phased out of conversations, and my mind pulls out a random afterthought from its quagmire of the ‘thought repository’, as I put it.

An inner voice echoes, “You see, all these people walking around the food court…are forms of life...LIFE…I don’t know how we define it…Is it an entropy accelerator that conforms to the 2nd law of thermodynamics? Do we even have a convincing unified definition that is agreeable to our planet and beyond?” This random thought segues into the point of me writing this emotionless “catharsis” at 2:50 AM on a Friday morning.

THE WAY WE DEFINE LIFE IS WRONG.

To be honest, it’s not really a definition. Life has to take in energy. Life has to reproduce. Life must respond to its environment. Life must consist of cells. Life should have respiration. This is not really a definition; it’s an attempt to draw a line…a set of boundaries around what we think life is. It’s even funny how half of these qualifying factors fit into your average 4-stroke IC engine car.

Though, it is expected for something so amorphous and complicated as life and, well…social constructs. But life is not a construct of our opinions. It’s a reality, rocks and stones alike. It’s a peculiar bubble of very interesting chemistry that forms and then pops…forms and then pops.

Life is a chemical system that uses energy to keep itself far from reaching chemical equilibrium. When that system ceases to exist, we die.ΔG ≠ 0 · for as long as it lasts

In retrospect, we often think we “die” every day. Literally and rhetorically, for some, apparently. But if we really put that into thought…it’s really not a lifelong continuum. We shed our skin every day, and the atoms and molecules that make it…return to equilibrium while the parent body that synthesised it continues living. We replace it with new ones every day. My RBCs get replaced, so does my skin, and we continue to live.

We technically die ONCE, and that happens when the system that maintains the “Far from equilibrium” state ceases to exist. Could be a myocardial infarction because your heart suddenly decides that it needs to take a dusshera break :) but for you, sir, That’s IT. You die. ONCE.

Death is the moment when the system that maintains the far-from-equilibrium state ceases to exist. We can imagine that on different scales, from individuals to species. For a Lactobacillus, it happens when the amino acids that make up its cell membrane fall apart. For a species, it’s when the last individual organism that constitutes that species dies. Extinction is also a form of death at different scales.

Whatever it might be, while be delve into the world of biology and tangentially scrape by the surface of existentialism, all that matters is that it that this peculiar system of interesting chemicals maintaining far from equilibrium must exist…pardon…THRIVE! It’s a gift, and one should relish it every moment, whether by getting giddy with delight at your first love or infatuation or a new form of personal discovery. Don’t let anyone’s pernickety zealot tell you otherwise, not even your strict inner self.

I’ll stop this chain of thought by quoting a phrase from Alan Watts,

“You can’t live at all unless you can live fully now.”
Alan Watts

Cheers!…
